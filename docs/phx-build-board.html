Today's questions

Stripe: new account, not piggyback

You may legally use Lytbub's account — Stripe's rule is one account per legal entity, and PHX is currently Lytbub Technologies LLC. But don't. The deciding reason: as a marketplace you must accept liability for negative balances, and Stripe can hold reserves on your platform account for up to 180 days. One bad month on PHX would freeze the cash that pays Lytbub's contractors.

Also: PHX is a "content platform" on Stripe's restricted list and needs pre-approval — you don't want that review touching a live account with client invoices in it. And a consumer seeing "LYTBUB TECH" on a $15 charge disputes it.

Cost of doing it right: $0 and about 2 hours. Same login, new account, AZ DBA for "PHX" (~$10), separate bank account. When PHX becomes its own entity later, Stripe changes the legal entity on that account — routine, because it only ever did PHX business. Migrating a live Connect platform later means every artist re-onboards.

One thing to do before Stripe underwriting: have your artist licensing agreement drafted. Unlicensed music distribution is prohibited, not merely restricted. Handing Stripe a signed sample agreement turns a scary review into a routine one.

Referral program — build it, double-sided, artists are the real lever

Buy nothing. A working referral system on your stack is 6–10 hours; the cheapest tool that fits is $49/mo to serve zero affiliates. Structure:

  • Both sides get a free month — one-sided asks people to spam friends for personal gain, which reads badly in a tight scene. Double-sided reframes it as a gift.
  • Reward fires on the referee's 2nd paid invoice, not signup. Costs a 30-day delay, removes the entire fraud surface.
  • Milestone ladder at 3 / 10 / 25. In the best available benchmark, 83% of successful referrers refer exactly once — and the top 1.1% drive 30% of all referrals. The whole game is giving your super-referrers somewhere to keep going.
  • Artist referral tier, Fourthwall-style: commission unlocks with the artist's own activity on PHX, not just referral volume. Only artists actually using PHX can earn from PHX.

The decision you have to make: when someone gets a comped month, does the artist still get paid? Say yes, fund it from your 50%, and say so publicly. It costs ~$7.50 per referral pair and it's a trust asset in a scene where everyone talks. Say no and you've built a growth mechanic that quietly hurts artists.

Tag a product in a post → it becomes a review ✅

This is the best idea in your message and you should build it. Three reasons it's stronger than it looks:

  • Customer photos are one of the highest-evidence conversion levers in apparel — Baymard finds a 63% navigation gap for them, and shoppers treat them as objective proof in a way they never treat brand photos.
  • You already have the hard parts: a social feed, media upload, and post→track attachment. Product tagging is the same mechanic pointed at a different table.
  • No POD store on earth has this, because they're stores, not social networks. Fourthwall can't build it. This is the merch feature that's actually defensible.

Build it as: post composer gets "tag a product" → creates a merch_reviews row linked to the post → the product page shows real fans wearing it, pulled live from the feed. Ships with the merch UI.

The Artist Hub idea — my honest read

You asked whether to build a paid Artist Hub: merch management, analytics from all their other platforms, social scheduling, sold in tiers. I had it researched hard, because it's the kind of idea that's expensive to be wrong about. The answer is don't build it as described — and two of the four pieces aren't "later," they're permanently blocked.

PieceVerdictWhy
Analytics from Spotify / AppleBlockedSpotify for Artists has no public API — confirmed by Spotify staff. The Web API needs 250,000 monthly users to leave dev mode, which caps you at 5 authenticated users; you'd hit the wall at artist #6. In Feb 2026 they also stripped follower and popularity fields. Apple Music's equivalent is labels-and-distributors only. This isn't hard, it's prohibited.
Social schedulingLoses moneyAt 30 artists the API aggregator alone is $599/mo — $20/artist in pure cost — to compete with Buffer's free tier and Metricool's free 50 posts/month. X now charges $0.20 per post containing a link, and artists post links constantly.
Merch managementAlready freeFourthwall gives musicians a full POD storefront for $0 and absorbs merchant-of-record liability. Building this to compete is building a worse free thing.
Charging artists tiersCosts you the pitchYour entire recruiting line is "free to upload, you earn more here, we make money from the fan side." Introducing artist subscriptions during the conversation where you're hand-recruiting 30 artists converts a gift into a transaction.

The market pattern is unusually clear. Every artist-direct tools company either died (ToneDen, shut down 2024 — the fanlink.to domain expired and every artist's links broke overnight), pivoted to selling B2B (Symphony went from $6–20/mo for artists to $99–799/mo for labels; Beatchain now white-labels to music businesses), went free and monetized transactions (Fourthwall, Bandsintown), or is being stripped for parts (Bandcamp, two rounds of layoffs). There are zero examples of a sustainable artist-pays-monthly tools business at indie scale.

And the money isn't there: per Spotify's own 2026 report, the 100,000th highest-earning artist on Earth made about $7,300 — gross, before the distributor cut. Your first 30 Phoenix artists are not in that group. Meanwhile B2C subscriptions under $25/mo churn at 8.2% monthly — you'd lose ~64% of subscribers a year.

The version that works — same instinct, different customer

Your instinct is right: PHX needs revenue that isn't just $15 consumer subscriptions. Three moves that use what you actually own:

  • Give artists a free dashboard on PHX's own data — which Phoenix neighborhoods their listeners are in, named contactable fans, which other local artists their listeners play. Spotify has this and will never share it. It costs you nothing, it's a recruiting weapon, and it strengthens the free pitch instead of undermining it.
  • Sell to venues and promoters, not artists. They have marketing budgets; artists have $47. "This artist has 340 active listeners within 5 miles of your venue" is the hardest question in local booking and only your data answers it. Bandsintown runs exactly this model — free for artists, paid for venues.
  • Local sponsorship, starting now. One Roosevelt Row venue at $500/mo equals 33 subscribers, with no Stripe fees, no artist payout, no churn. For comparison: Axios Local converts free readers to paid at 0.75%, while 6AM City is profitable at $9.5M revenue on essentially zero consumer subscriptions — it's all local advertising.

If you ever do charge artists, charge for outcomes — a showcase slot, featured placement, a co-op ad buy — never for software.
