# PHX — Ownership & Partnership Framework

> STATUS: current as of 2026-08-07 — discussion framework, not executed
_A framework for who owns what and who gets paid what, so the people building and fronting PHX are treated fairly before it grows. **This is a discussion framework, not legal or financial advice — an attorney (startup/entertainment) and an accountant should paper anything before signatures.**_

---

## The situation (as stated)

- **Lightbulb (Lytbub) Studios** — Jayem's company — is building and operating the entire platform, solo.
- **The faces / rollout partners:** **Murkemz** (headline artist), **Bryce Breeze** (management/team), **Plug City** (record label). They'll likely drive rollout, scene credibility, and marketing.
- Terms among the parties are **not yet decided.** Jayem wants them fair to everyone.

## The five principles I'd build the deal on

**1. Separate the *technology* from the *venture*.**
Lightbulb Studios built and owns the platform — the code, the brand, the IP. That doesn't automatically become shared property because partners help launch it. Decide deliberately: the tech/IP sits in an entity Jayem controls; the *venture* (rolling PHX out in Phoenix and beyond) is what partners get a stake in. Blurring these two is the #1 way this gets messy later.

**2. Tie reward to contribution — and make it vest.**
The person doing the building and bearing the risk (Jayem) holds **control and the majority.** Partners who bring the scene, the name, and the marketing get a **real, *defined* slice** — but earned over time (vesting), not granted in full on day one. If someone drifts after the launch push, their stake shouldn't keep growing. Vesting protects everyone: it rewards people who actually stay in it.

**3. Handle the double-hats — this is the subtle one.**
Several people wear two hats, and the money for each must stay separate:
- **Murkemz** is both a *possible owner* AND an *artist who gets paid by the payout engine.* His artist earnings are not his ownership stake, and vice versa. He'd get paid as an artist exactly like every other artist, regardless of any ownership.
- **Plug City** is both a *venture partner* AND a *label whose artists list on the platform.* Its artists get normal artist terms; the label's partner stake is separate. Guard against Plug City artists getting preferential payout treatment — that would poison the "fair to every artist" promise.
- **Bryce Breeze** is both a *partner* AND potentially an *operational role* (management/roster). If they do real ongoing work, that can be compensated as a role (comp/salary/rev-share) *in addition to* any equity — but define it.

**4. Founder control stays with the builder.**
Whatever the split, Jayem should keep decision control (majority voting, or a controlling class) while he's the one building and operating. Partners can have economic upside without operational control — that's normal and fair, because the day-to-day risk and work are his.

**5. Everything real gets papered *before* traction.**
The moment money or a following shows up, a handshake becomes a dispute. Before that: form the entity, assign the IP into it, and sign a founders'/partnership agreement with vesting. Cheap now, very expensive later.

## Structure options for the partners' stake (pick per person)

| Model | What it is | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity (vesting)** | A % of the PHX entity, earned over 2–4 years | Partners committed long-term to building the venture |
| **Revenue share** | A defined % of platform net revenue, capped or perpetual | Partners who contribute reach/marketing but not ownership risk |
| **Founding-partner / ambassador deal** | Fixed comp + perks + title, no ownership | Faces who lend name/credibility for the launch window |
| **Role compensation** | Pay for actual ongoing work (ops, A&R, management) | Anyone doing real recurring labor |

Most likely you mix these — e.g., a modest **vesting equity** slice for the core faces, **role comp** for whoever does ongoing work, and **normal artist terms** layered on top for Murkemz/Plug City artists.

## An *illustrative* shape (not a recommendation of numbers — a way to think)

- **Lightbulb Studios / Jayem:** majority + control (built it, operates it, bears the risk).
- **A partner pool** for the faces (Murkemz, Bryce Breeze, Plug City), split by contribution and **vesting over time**.
- **An option pool** reserved for future team/artists you'll want to reward.
- Artist payouts (including Murkemz) run through the normal engine, **outside** the cap table.

The exact percentages are yours + your attorney's call, informed by who actually does what — which is why the roles matrix below matters more than a number.

## What to paper (checklist for your attorney)

1. **Entity formation** (AZ LLC or C-corp — ask counsel; C-corp if you ever raise money).
2. **IP assignment** — all PHX code/brand/IP assigned *into* the entity (so it's owned cleanly, not personally).
3. **Founders'/partnership agreement** — the split, vesting schedule, control, what happens if someone leaves (buy-sell), and that IP stays with the company.
4. **Separate artist agreements** — the in-app Artist Distribution Agreement already exists; keep artist terms fully separate from ownership.
5. **Role/comp agreements** — for anyone doing ongoing paid work.

## The awareness flags (you asked me to raise these)

- **Do the paperwork before the launch push, not after.** The rollout is when a following appears — that's the worst time to still be undefined.
- **Keep artist payouts sacred and separate from ownership.** If partners-who-are-also-artists get special payout treatment, the "fair to every Phoenix artist" brand — your whole moat — cracks.
- **Vest everything.** Never grant full stakes up front for a launch that hasn't happened yet.
- **You're the builder — keep control.** Generosity on *economics* is fine; giving up *control* while you're the one doing the work is the mistake to avoid.
- **This needs a real attorney + accountant.** I can frame the deal; I can't make it legal. Entertainment/startup counsel who's seen artist-platform deals is worth every penny here.
