Membership & Merch Setup

How a new person becomes a member, what they pay and when, and the exact step-by-step for setting up an artist's print-on-demand store as admin.

PART ONEHow membership works

You can't charge $15/month for six tracks — there's nothing to listen to yet. But free-forever kills the price anchor and leaves artists earning $0. The answer is neither a trial nor a free tier: it's a founding membership that's free now and priced forever.

The three phases

Foundingnow → catalog fills

Invite-only. $0. Full Native access. Everyone who redeems a code claims a permanent founding number (#1, #2, #3…) and locks a price — $9.99/mo Native — that they'll pay later and keep for life. No card at signup.

Capped at 1,000. The cap is what does the selling: scarcity plus a permanent number is a much better offer than "free beta."

Convertingwhen the catalog earns it

Billing turns on. Founding members are asked for a card at their locked $9.99, not at $15. Anyone who declines drops to Explorer (free) — they don't lose their account, just the Native features.

Publicopen signup

$15 Native / $29 Insider. Founding members keep $9.99 forever — that's the whole point, and it's why the early cohort evangelises.

Why not a free trial. Trials only work when the product is already good enough to convert on. Right now the catalog is the product, and it's six tracks — a 14-day clock would expire before there's a reason to pay, and you'd have burned the person. Worse, a card-required trial at the door would gut signups from a cold Instagram link, and a cardless trial converts at 15–25%. The founding structure sidesteps the whole problem: no clock, no card, and the conversion moment arrives when the catalog actually justifies it.

What a new person actually experiences

Taps Murkemz's IG bio link → /a/murkemz — real page, real preview card, plays music logged out → likes it, hits "Claim your pass" → enters code QVRPRA (Murkemz's code) → picks a @username → "You're PHX Native #7 — locked at $9.99/mo, forever" → full access. Pays nothing today.

The part that makes it work for artists

The founding pool. During the free phase nobody pays, so wallets are $0, so artists earn $0 — which kills the artist pitch at the exact moment you're recruiting the first thirty. So PHX seeds money into the pot directly. Artists then earn real dollars from real listening before a single subscriber exists, through the same capped, fraud-checked machinery as everything else.

It's a marketing cost with a receipt attached — and "we paid Phoenix artists $X before we charged anyone a dollar" is a better recruiting line than anything you could say about the product.

A trap I hit while testing this, and fixed. I simulated a $500 seed against your current 12 streams and it paid $41 per stream. Great first cheque — and a disaster, because when real subscribers arrive the rate reverts to ~0.75¢ and every artist watches their earnings collapse. Ramping up reads as momentum; crashing down reads as a bait-and-switch. So the seed now pays at most 7.5¢/stream (10× the normal rate — generous, same order of magnitude), and whatever the catalog can't absorb simply isn't spent. Re-run with the ceiling: Murkemz $0.53, Jaye Mali $0.38 on 12 streams. Small, honest, and it only goes up.

The number is yours to set. It's currently 0 — nothing pays out until you choose. $200–500/month is the range I'd suggest for the founding phase: enough that a working artist notices, small enough that it's a rounding error against the story it buys.

Rewards

  • Double-sided — both people earn PHX Points. One-sided asks someone to spam their friends for personal gain, which reads badly in a tight scene; double-sided makes it a gift.
  • Earned now — referral credit (points, perks, milestones) accrues during the free phase. No free months, ever — membership pricing is never discounted by referrals.
  • Qualifies after 30 days plus real listening (founding phase) or a settled invoice (billing phase). Never at signup — that's the entire fraud surface.
  • Milestone ladder at 3 / 10 / 25 — three months free, then a founding Insider upgrade, then guest list plus a PHX metal card. This exists because ~83% of referrers refer exactly once while the top ~1% drive ~30% of all referrals; the ladder gives those people somewhere to keep going.

Fraud guards

  • Self-referral blocked outright.
  • One referral per person, ever — enforced at the database level.
  • Device-fingerprint collisions flag for review rather than block, so a shared family laptop isn't punished, but nothing pays out until it's cleared.
  • Rewards run through an admin-only function — the client can never trigger a payout.

PART THREESetting up merch — the full walkthrough

you do this in PHX admin

Path A — Print-on-demand (dropshipping)

This is the Murkemz path. Nobody holds inventory; a shirt gets printed only when someone buys one.

1
Create the Fourthwall shop — you, not the artistfourthwall.com, free, ~15 minutes. Name it for the artist. You own the login; the artist never has to touch it. This is deliberate for now — every store meets a quality bar, which is the point of a curated platform.
2
Upload the artwork300 DPI PNG, transparent background. Fourthwall's Merch Maker places it on the product and shows you a mockup. You can generate designs or reuse what's already on murkmerch.com.
3
Pick products and set pricesTheir catalog is far wider than tees — hoodies, crewnecks, hats, beanies, joggers, tote bags, mugs, posters, stickers, phone cases, blankets, socks. Base costs: Gildan tee $9.50, Bella+Canvas tee $11.75, Comfort Colors hoodie $26.95, mug $5.95. You set retail; everything above base is the artist's. A $28 tee on an $11.75 blank leaves ~$16.
4
Order one sample of eachNever sell something you haven't held. Also gives you a real photo, which converts better than any mockup.
5
Copy the Storefront tokenFourthwall → Settings → For Developers → Storefront token. It's a public read token, but it goes in the admin-only artist_merch_secrets table, never on the artists row — that's the leak I fixed earlier.
6
Switch the artist on in PHX adminSet merch_mode = 'fourthwall_headless' and paste the token. PHX's server-side proxy fetches their products and renders them natively on the artist page in PHX styling — the customer never sees a Fourthwall page until checkout.
7
Done — it's liveProducts appear on /a/murkemz. Buy button hands off to Fourthwall's checkout.

What happens when someone buys

Fan taps Buy on the PHX artist page → Fourthwall checkout (card / PayPal / Apple Pay) → Fourthwall charges them — they are the merchant of record → supplier prints and ships, 7–10 days, artist's branding → artist gets everything above base cost → PHX earns partner commission on net sales Sales tax, chargebacks, refunds, customer support: all Fourthwall's
Why this structure and not Printful direct. Printful's API is better, but with them you become the merchant of record — which means you personally own sales-tax nexus in every state you cross a threshold in, plus returns, chargebacks, and support. As an Arizona-based marketplace you'd owe TPT from the first sale. Letting Fourthwall be merchant of record removes all of it, and their Partner Dashboard is purpose-built for exactly your shape: one operator running many creator shops, with commission paid to the operator. Apply for it once Murkemz's shop is live — you need one live shop to qualify.

Path B — Direct-ship (the artist's own inventory)

Vinyl, cassettes, hand-made pieces, chains, anything they already have boxes of. The schema supports this today — fulfilment = 'direct_ship'.

1
You create the product in PHX adminName, price, photos, variants, stock count, and a ship note — "Ships from Phoenix in 2–3 days." Stock decrements on order.
2
Order comes in → the artist ships itThey get notified, pack it, mark it shipped. For beta, keep this to artists you trust to actually ship — the failure mode is a fan waiting on someone flaky while PHX takes the blame.

Also supported: digital (download/unlock) and pickup (collect at a show — genuinely useful for a local platform, and zero shipping cost).

The thing that makes PHX merch different

Fans tag products in posts, and it becomes a review. Someone posts a photo wearing the hoodie, tags the product, and it shows up as social proof on the product page with their photo.

Customer photos are one of the highest-evidence conversion levers in apparel, and no print-on-demand store can build this — they're stores, not social networks. Fourthwall structurally can't. You already have the feed, the media upload, and the tagging mechanic. The tables are in; it ships with the merch UI.
